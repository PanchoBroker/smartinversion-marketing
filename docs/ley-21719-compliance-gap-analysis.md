## 1. Why this document exists, and what it is not

The product owner asked for a careful read of Ley 21.719 and an assessment of how to adapt the project to it, to avoid legal consequences. This document is that assessment: verified facts from the law's actual text, cross-referenced against the project's real files (not assumed). It is not a legal opinion — only a Chilean lawyer can tell you whether a specific implementation satisfies the law. Where this document uses "should" or flags a gap, that is a technical/product reading of the statute, not legal certification.

## 2. Key facts verified from the law's text (2026-08-11)

- **Entry into force: 2026-12-01.** Confirmed directly in the law's transitory provisions (`Artículo primero` transitorio: 24 months after publication in the Diario Oficial, 2024-12-13) — matches the date already used in `docs/d06-d07-consent-retention-draft-proposal.md` §1.1.
- **Correction to the earlier draft's breach-notification claim.** `docs/d06-d07-consent-retention-draft-proposal.md` §1.1 (written 2026-08-10) described the reform as including "72-hour breach notification." Having now read Article 14 sexies directly: the law does **not** fix a 72-hour clock. It requires reporting to the Agency "por los medios más expeditos posibles y sin dilaciones indebidas" (undue delay, no fixed number), and additionally requires notifying affected titulares directly when the breach involves sensitive data, data of children under 14, or economic/financial/banking/commercial data. The 72-hour figure appears to have been carried over from GDPR by association, not read from this law. Worth fixing in that draft the next time it's touched (not urgent — the draft was explicit that its facts should be verified, and this document now does that).
- **Sanctions are tiered by severity**, not a flat maximum: leves up to 5,000 UTM (warning or fine), graves up to 10,000 UTM, gravísimas up to 20,000 UTM (Art. 35). For a company that is not "menor tamaño" under Ley 20.416 and repeats a grave/gravísima infraction, the fine can instead reach 2% (graves) or 4% (gravísimas) of annual revenue if that is higher (Art. 35, inciso final).
- **First-12-months grace period for small/medium companies** (Art. sexto transitorio): during the first 12 months after 2026-12-01, if a company classified as "menor tamaño" under Ley 20.416 would otherwise be fined, the Agency may instead apply only a written warning. This is real but narrow — it reduces first-offense financial risk for a company Smartinversion's likely size, it does not exempt anything, and it doesn't apply to gravísimas the Agency chooses to treat more severely, or to reincidencia.
- **A data-protection officer ("delegado de protección de datos") is voluntary**, not mandatory (Art. 49-50). For a micro/small/medium company, the owner can personally take the role. Having one (plus a documented compliance program) is an explicit mitigating factor when the Agency calculates a fine (Art. 36 N°5) — worth considering as a cheap risk-reduction step, not a legal requirement.
- **International data transfer has real conditions** (Título V, Art. 27-29): lawful only when the destination country has an Agency-recognized "adequate" protection level (a list the Agency has not published yet — it doesn't exist until the Agency is fully operational after 2026-12-01), or under contractual clauses/binding corporate rules/certification with adequate guarantees, or under one of the listed exceptions — the most relevant one here being **contract necessity**: "cuando la transferencia resulte necesaria para la celebración o ejecución de un contrato entre el titular y el responsable" (Art. 27, inciso segundo, letra g). Explicit consent to a specific transfer is also an exception (letra a).

## 3. What the project already does right, by design

Cross-referencing `docs/preliminary-form-contract.md` (S0-015, the normative source for the public lead form) against the law found deliberate alignment already built in, before this document existed:

- **Purpose limitation and data minimization** (Art. 3 letters b/c): §5 explicitly forbids collecting RUT, DICOM, debts, banking credentials, payslips, tax documents, or any data beyond what the marketing prefilter needs. This matches the law's proportionality principle almost verbatim.
- **No bundled consent** (relevant to Art. 12's "free" requirement): §19 already states "Unrelated optional marketing permissions MUST NOT be bundled into one mandatory checkbox" — this anticipates Art. 12's presumption that consent is not freely given when collected as an unnecessary condition of a contract/service.
- **Consent evidence structure** matches Art. 12's requirements almost element-for-element: notice version, notice-text hash, server-authoritative timestamp, purpose, submission reference (§19) — this is the same list `docs/decision-register.md` D-06 already fixed as its "Current direction," now confirmed to line up with what the statute actually asks for.
- **No PII in technical logs** (§26-27): consistent with Art. 14 quinquies' security duty and general confidentiality principle.
- **Retention left undefined on purpose** (D-07): consistent with Art. 3 letter c, which requires retention "only for the period necessary" but — like Gate G0 already decided — does not hand you a number either.
